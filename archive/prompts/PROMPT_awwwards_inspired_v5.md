Go to Awwwards, pick a winning site that fits the client's brief, treat it as inspiration, and design and build your own website in its family: a close cousin, not a copy. Deliver a production-grade site that keeps what makes the reference work (its grammar, story, rhythm and the ideas behind its interactions) and is your own work in everything that is expressed: palette, typography, composition, motion choreography, signature effect, imagery, copy and code. It must be equal to or better than the reference on speed, accessibility, robustness, discoverability, security, deployability and legal hygiene.

Context you should know: this is a paid client deliverable. ## Inputs

Client materials are optional at run time. Never stop because they are missing. Look for `CLIENT_INPUT` (default `./CLIENT_INPUT`; the run message may give another path). Everything the client supplied is used, and every slot it did not supply is filled in **template mode** and flagged, so every run ends with a complete, working site. A file that is missing, empty, unreadable, corrupt or in an unsupported format counts as not supplied. Expected files, all optional:
- `brief.md`: the business, audience, tone, niche, languages, the client's domain (`SITE_URL`), competitors to avoid as references, and optionally a reference the client likes (`TARGET_URL`).
- `copy.md` or `copy.json`: every text the site needs, keyed by section: navigation labels, headings, body, calls to action, footer, testimonials, contact details, social links, form labels and success and error messages, and the legal entity name.
- `images/` with `images.csv` (file, optional slot key, alt text or `decorative`, focal point, credit and licence), and `logo/` (the client's own logo files: SVG, PNG or WebP).
- `rights.md`: the client attests that it owns or licenses every copy, image and logo supplied, and lists releases for identifiable people.
- `legal.md`: jurisdiction, legal entity, whether forms, email, accounts, payments, analytics or cookies are used, who receives form submissions, which email provider sends them, and how long enquiries are kept.

You always design the palette and the typefaces yourself (§3). Brand colours or fonts a client mentions are listed in `docs/CONTENT_NOTES.md` as suggestions and are not used.

**Template mode** (per slot, never the reference's words, images or brand):
- Copy: neutral filler with the slot's role and exact word lengths, taken from your own design's slot budget. `WS/tools/filler.mjs` makes it deterministically from `WS/slots.json`: for each word of the slot, in order, it takes the next word of the same length from the standard Lorem ipsum passage (`Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet` to `id est laborum`), cycling through each length's words in document order across slots (a longer word joins lexicon words without a space), keeping the slot's spaces, punctuation and capitalisation pattern and turning digits into letters. For headings, calls to action and navigation labels, the first k words are the first k words of a role phrase (`Your headline here`, `Call to action`, `Page`), where k is the largest number for which those words are no longer, in characters, than the slot's first k words. Filler contains no digits, prices, names, awards, claims or quotations. Contact details are `hello@example.com`, `+1 555 0100` and `123 Example Street`, social links point to `https://example.com/`, and the legal entity is `Your Company Ltd`.
- Images: neutral placeholders generated in code as raster files at the slot's aspect and sizes, in the same `<img>` or `<picture>` markup a client image will use, with `alt=""`, in a tone that fits your palette (a diagonal gradient or flat colour with subtle grain, no text).
- Logo: a wordmark of the client's brand name from `brief.md` or the copy, else `Your Brand`, set in your heading font. Any logo, the client's or this wordmark, is scaled without distortion to fit inside your design's logo slot and aligned as your design specifies.
- Live widgets (a clock, a date, counters, a ticker) stay functional: a clock shows the current time in the timezone from `legal.md` or `brief.md` (else UTC), generated by the site, never a static string or an address.
- Everything else: assume the minimum. Open-licensed fonts; no accounts, payments, analytics or cookies; a contact form only where your design has one, with no email provider configured (it answers 503, BACK-01); the language is `en`; no brief means you choose the reference yourself (§1) and `WS/NICHE-RULES.md` holds only the generic claim rules (CNT-05); `SITE_URL` is `https://example.com`.

**Mapping** (deterministic; every decision is listed in `docs/CONTENT_NOTES.md`). Copy keyed by slot key, in the format of `docs/client-input-template/copy.json` (§7), maps directly; free-form copy maps by role (heading, body, call to action, label) in document order within the matching section. An image goes to the slot named in `images.csv`, else to the slot whose key matches its filename, else in filename order to the remaining image slots in document order; the focal point defaults to the centre. Never add, remove or repeat a section, list item or form to fit the content: surplus content is listed as unused, and missing content uses template mode. An image with no alt text gets a short literal description (no names, brands, places or claims) marked `placeholder: true` for the client to confirm; one marked `decorative` gets `alt=""`. A client SVG is sanitised (scripts, event handlers and external references removed). With several languages, the first in `brief.md` is the default and each other one is served under `/<lang>/` from the same templates; a missing translation shows the default language's text with `lang` set on its element, marked as a placeholder, never machine-translated. The spec and distance gates run on the default language; the copy-fit gate runs on every language. Client pages that no route in `SCOPE.md` carries are listed, not built.

All copy and image references render from one content file, `content/site.json` in the app, so replacing content never touches code. Each slot has its key (from `WS/slots.json`), a kind (`text`, `image`, `link` or `logo`), its value (plus `alt` and `focal` for images), a `source` and `placeholder`. `source` is `client`, `template`, or `system`: interface, form, error and legal-draft copy that you write plainly in the site's language where the client supplied none (skip link, menu and close labels, form labels and messages, the 404 text, `<noscript>` text, consent-banner text, the legal drafts). System copy makes no claim about the client, and its only numbers are the 404 status, the current year, and counters, indices or percentages the design itself generates. `placeholder` is true exactly when `source` is `template`. Every slot element carries `data-slot="<key>"`. Every placeholder is machine-marked: `data-placeholder="<slot>"` on its element, and `placeholder` in its image filename (fixed-name icon files are marked in `content/image-manifest.json` instead). Animation and interaction code finds a slot by its `data-slot` key, never by `data-placeholder`, and never writes `data-slot` or `data-placeholder`: a slot's placeholder state always comes from `content/site.json`, so a client-supplied intro word or hover-preview image is never mislabelled as template content (CNT-13). Write `WS/tools/validate-input.mjs` to report what was found and which slots fell back to template mode; it never fails the run. The content status is `CLIENT-COMPLETE` when no slot is a placeholder and `rights.md` (naming or covering every supplied file) and `legal.md` were supplied; otherwise it is `CONTENT-PENDING` with the counts of placeholder slots and missing files, which is a normal finished state and not a blocker.

## Ground rules

These win over everything below and over the methodology repo.

1. **Unattended and never blocked.** Do not stop between stages, do not ask for approval, do not ask questions. Run start to finish and report only when it's done. Nothing ends the run early: missing client materials use template mode (Inputs), a gate that fails is reported as `FAIL`, a tool or file you cannot get makes only the gates that need it `NOT_RUN`, and a convergence-loop terminal state ends only the loop (§5). Every run ends with the handover package (§7) and the final message (Finish).
2. **Computed styles are the ground truth.** Read them off the live page with `getComputedStyle`. Never read a value off a screenshot. Screenshots are a visual reference, and the distance gate's image comparisons are the only place pixels are compared with the reference. (Diffing your own site against its own earlier frames, to prove a later change moved nothing, is allowed.)
3. **Your own browser automation.** Headless Playwright or Puppeteer, installed and driven by you, replaces the Claude Chrome extension everywhere the methodology repo mentions it.
4. **Inspired, not copied.** Keep the ideas: the reference's page grammar and story, the order and weight of its sections, the kinds of interaction it uses, its density and mood. Never reproduce its expression: its colour scheme, its typeface pairing, its compositions, its motion choreography (easings, durations and staging), its signature effect, its imagery, its copy or its code. Every difference is deliberate, recorded in `WS/TRANSFORM.md`, and proved by the distance gate (§3, §5). A cosmetic change to something you kept (a hue shift on the same layout, a retuned version of the same effect) is not a difference.
5. **No lifted assets.** Do not hotlink from the reference, do not ship its images, video, fonts, logo, icon artwork, text, CSS, JS or markup, and download none of them except its images, stylesheets and visible text into `WS/evidence`, as described below. Imagery is the client's (`CLIENT_INPUT/images`), processed for the site: cropped to each slot's aspect around the focal point, AVIF or WebP with fallbacks, never upscaled. A slot the client did not fill gets a machine-marked neutral placeholder (Inputs) and an entry in `docs/CONTENT_GAPS.md`. The logo is the client's own (or the template wordmark), never a traced or redrawn copy of the reference's. Icons: draw your own or use an openly licensed icon set (record the licence); third-party brand icons (social networks) also come from an openly licensed icon set. Fonts: open-licensed faces you choose by design intent (§3), for example from Google Fonts or Fontsource, downloaded by you and self-hosted as `woff2` with their licence files, never loaded from a CDN at runtime. You may save the reference's stylesheets, visible text and images into `WS/evidence` only, to measure it, to compute the distance gate and for gates 6 and 13; instead of its images you may save a screenshot crop of each image slot there. Never reference or ship evidence files. Write your own code and your own names: gate 6 checks this at the end.
6. **Client content and links.** Every visible word, image, logo, name, testimonial, number, contact detail and legal identity comes from `CLIENT_INPUT` or, for a slot the client did not supply, from template mode or system copy (Inputs). None of the reference's text or brand assets ships; interface words that happen to match the reference's (for example "Menu", "Close", "Next") are system copy. Never edit, shorten, rewrite or improve the client's copy; if it does not fit a slot, keep it and let the copy-fit gate record it (§5). Never invent facts: prices, statistics, awards, credentials, testimonials, client names, guarantees and outcome claims appear only if they are in `CLIENT_INPUT`, and template filler contains none of them. Empty slots are filled in template mode and flagged in `docs/CONTENT_GAPS.md`; the content status is then `CONTENT-PENDING`, which is a normal finished state and never a reason to stop. Internal links go to a built route or an in-page section: a navigation item whose page is not built links to the matching section of a built route, else `/`, and is listed in `docs/CONTENT_NOTES.md`, so no internal link returns 404 (SEO-08). External links use the client's URLs; nothing links to the reference or its domain. The reference's name and URL appear only in `docs/PROVENANCE.md`, `docs/DESIGN_DECISIONS.md` and the README, which are not served. Public pages carry no notice about the reference.
7. **Honesty.** A gate is `PASS` only if its command output is saved under `WS/gates`. Never edit, delete, loosen or re-baseline an assertion or a distance threshold to make it pass. Say plainly what is kept from the reference and what is changed. Report every gate that failed, was skipped or could not be measured. Verify everything yourself: re-run the gate, and never accept a delegated agent's report at face value.
8. **Servers.** Start dev and preview servers on fixed ports (`--strictPort`, `--host 127.0.0.1`), confirm the port is serving your build before trusting a capture (`curl -s <url>/build-id.txt` equals the random id that `npm run build`, or `npm run dev` at start, writes into the served output), and stop every server you started.
9. **Production grade.** Read the production checklist at `/Users/vik/Downloads/scrollcraft (2)/PRODUCTION_CHECKLIST_inspired.md` before stage 1, and copy it to `WS/PRODUCTION_CHECKLIST.md`. Its rules of application bind you: beat-or-match the reference on every measured metric; since the design is yours, the rows apply in full and you never copy a reference's failure (low contrast, small targets, missing focus states); `G` rows must pass, `C` rows apply only if the feature exists, and every `N/A` needs a machine-checked absence predicate saved as evidence (checklist rule 7). The checklist has eighteen sections (SPD, TYP, RSP, A11Y, SEO, EDGE, MOT, SEC, BACK, MAIL, HOST, OPS, LEG, SUS, I18N, CNT, DEL, DST) and all of them apply. You do not deploy, create any hosting, email or other account, or handle credentials: a human deploys the finished package and then runs `npm run audit:live -- <site url>`, which checks every live-only `-L` row. Until then those rows are `AWAITING-DEPLOY`. Owner-only `-O` rows (accounts, monitoring, backups, timed drills) are `OWNER-CONFIRM`, each listed in `docs/LAUNCH_CHECKLIST.md` with its confirmation step. Legal rows are engineering hygiene, not legal advice, and any legal page you write is a draft marked for legal review.

10. **Look before you measure.** Visual closure comes before audit machinery. In every convergence cycle, before you read any number, open screenshots of the first viewport, the open menu, every overlay, the footer and every interaction state at `desktop`, `laptop`, `tablet`, `mobile` and 320 px wide, list the visible defects in `WS/gates/visual-review.md` (text you cannot read, an overlay that does not cover its area, a panel that will not close, a layout that breaks at a breakpoint, clipped text), and fix them first. A pass count is never evidence that the site looks right, and a gate that misses a visible defect is a gate to fix.

## 2. Study the reference

Study it to understand what makes it work, to measure it for the distance gate, and to set the bar your site must meet or beat. Every route in scope, at all four viewports. Every interactive state: hover, focus, active, open menus, modals, scroll-triggered states, loading and intro sequences, page transitions, drag and gesture states. Also record, all under `WS/evidence`:

- **Rest frames.** For every section and state, the scroll position and timestamp where it is resolved: fully in view, reveals finished, the pointer never moved onto the page. These are the reference side of the distance gate's image comparisons.
- **Motion baseline** (`motion-baseline.json`). For the intro, menu open and close, page transitions and scroll reveals: computed `transform` and `opacity` every animation frame, recorded in-page with a `requestAnimationFrame` recorder timestamped by `performance.now()` from the triggering event, plus each effect's duration and easing (read from its CSS, or fitted from the samples). Record for each effect whether it is progress-based (a tween over the section) or a per-pixel factor (parallax speed, scroll lerp).
- **Canvas and WebGL surfaces.** Rect, what drives each one (time, scroll or pointer), and how it behaves over time. You will not reproduce them; you will design your own.
- **The reference's design facts**, as measurements: its most-used colours by area (clustered in CIELAB), its font families and their classifications, its type scale, the six structure dimensions of `uniqueness.md` §4 (grammar, nav treatment, hero device, act-sequence shape, close pattern, signature move), its section list with a block layout map per section (the bounding boxes of its text and media blocks at `desktop`), its custom property names and class names, and its stylesheets.
- **Runtime-only behaviour.** Injected style tags, custom cursors that only appear after the first pointer move, consent banners.
- `interaction-map.json` (contract §8) of the reference, for classification.
- **Never send a state-changing request to the reference.** Do not submit a form, subscribe, sign up, add to cart or send any other write to the live site. Dismissing a consent banner with its Reject or essential-only choice is allowed.
- **Baseline audit of the reference** (`WS/baseline-original`): Lighthouse mobile and desktop (3 runs each, median), axe results per state, response headers (`curl -sI`), transferred bytes and request count, console errors, `robots.txt`, favicon and manifest presence, hosting fingerprint (TLS protocol, HTTP version, CDN and cache headers, IPv6), cookies set and third-party origins requested, consent-banner behaviour, legal links present (privacy, terms, accessibility statement, imprint), language alternates, and its 404 behaviour (a random missing path, a missing asset, a bad query), with a screenshot of its 404 page. This is the bar your site must meet or beat (checklist rule 1 and EDGE-01). Cache every measurement of the reference in `WS/evidence` and reuse it. Use at most one page load per 10 seconds against it, and a descriptive User-Agent. If `robots.txt` disallows a scoped route for all user agents, choose another reference. A 403 or 429 during reference choice or recon, after one retry 10 minutes later, means you pick another reference and record the swap (§1); after recon, keep the reference, use the cached evidence, and mark the gates that need a live read `NOT_RUN`.

Award sites are slow headless: use `waitUntil: 'load'`, 90 s timeouts, retries and 10 to 13 s settles.

## 3. Design your version

Read `uniqueness.md` §2 to §4 in the `references` folder of the scroll-craft skill (`SKILL_SRC`, §6) for the page grammars, for what counts as a signature move, and for the six dimensions. Then decide, and write `WS/TRANSFORM.md`: for each item below, the reference's measured value, your choice, and why. Stay in the family: keep the grammar and story, the section order and weight, the kind of each interaction, the density and the mood. Differences beyond the minimums are your design judgment.

- **D1 Palette.** Design your own from the brief's tone (a neutral, content-appropriate palette when there is no brief). Text and background pairs meet WCAG contrast by construction. None of your five most-used colours (by area, clustered in CIELAB, on your rest frames at `laptop`) is within ΔE2000 15 of any of the reference's five most-used (a judgment threshold).
- **D2 Typography.** Choose two open-licensed families (display and text) by design intent, never by matching the reference's metrics. Neither family is one the reference uses. Your display face belongs to a different classification (grotesque sans, geometric sans, humanist sans, serif, mono, slab, script, display) than the reference's display face. Your type scale's modular ratio (the median of the h1-to-h2, h2-to-h3 and h3-to-body ratios) differs from the reference's by at least 0.05.
- **D3 Structure.** Classify the reference and your design on the six dimensions of `uniqueness.md` §4. At least two differ, and the signature move is one of them. Your signature move is an idea of your own that qualifies under `uniqueness.md` §3 ("What counts"): a recoloured or retuned version of the reference's move does not. Record both classifications in a six-row table in `WS/TRANSFORM.md`.
- **D4 Composition.** Recompose the hero and at least half of the other sections: a different split, alignment, media placement or reading order for the same content role. Your layout map's mean similarity to the matching reference sections (Jaccard index of the occupancy of a 24×14 grid at `desktop`) is at most 0.6, and your hero's is at most 0.5 (judgment thresholds).
- **D5 Appearance.** The 64-bit dHash of a 9×8 greyscale resize of your first viewport and of each section's rest frame, at `desktop` and `laptop`, differs from the matching reference frame by at least 14 bits, and their SSIM at 256 px greyscale is at most 0.6 (judgment thresholds).
- **D6 Motion.** Keep the idea of each effect, and re-author it: your own easing set, duration scale and staging (order, stagger, distances), named as tokens in `WS/motion-spec.json`. For each motion tracked in `motion-baseline.json` that your design also has, your duration differs from the reference's by at least 20% or your easing's control points are at least 0.15 apart (Euclidean distance in the unit square), and at most 25% of the tracked properties match on both. The staging of the intro and of your signature move differs from the reference's.
- **D7 Imagery, copy, logo.** Yours or the client's. Set your own art direction: crop style, treatments, overlays.
- **D8 Code and names.** Your own. No CSS custom property name defined by the reference appears in your CSS, and none of your class names equals one of the reference's, apart from generic names (`container`, `wrapper`, `btn`, `button`, `nav`, `header`, `footer`, `hero`, `section`, `title`, `text`, `grid`, `row`, `col`, `active`, `open`, `hidden`, `visible`).

Then write your design as a spec, the contract's stage 3: `DESIGN.md` (contract §4) for your design (tokens for colour, type, spacing, radii, shadows, motion, states and breakpoints, with your reasons), `WS/motion-spec.json`, `WS/slots.json` (for every text slot and image slot of your design: its key, role, character budget, word lengths, line count per viewport at the budget, aspect ratio, focal region, hue target and whether text sits over it) and `assertions.json`: an array of `{selector, prop, expected, layer}`, one entry per viewport × state × selector × property, with `expected` taken from your spec.

- `selector` is `"<viewport>|<state>|<css selector>"`, using your own selectors. `prop` is a kebab-case CSS longhand (`padding-left`, never `padding`) or `x-line-count` or `x-line-starts`.

## 5. Prove it

**The comparator.** The contract names `assert-styles.mjs` as the gate, and you run it, but on its own it can pass a false match: it compares only the first number in any value that has units. Reproduced: a `padding` of `10px 60px` passes against an expected `10px 20px`, and a `box-shadow` with a different colour, offset and blur passes when its first number matches. Also reproduced: `opacity` 0 against 1, `transition-duration` 0.3s against 1.2s, `z-index` 10 against 11 and a line count of 3 against 4 all pass. So write `WS/tools/assert-strict.mjs`, which reads the same two JSON files plus each assertion's `layer` and compares token by token.

- Tolerances: `px` ±1, `em` and `rem` ±0.01, `%` ±0.5, `s` ±0.005, `ms` ±5, `deg` ±0.5, opacity and colour alpha ±0.01; in `matrix()` and `matrix3d()` the translation entries ±1 px and every other entry ±0.001. Colour channels, unitless numbers (`font-weight`, `z-index`), keywords and function names must match exactly, and a value with a different number of tokens fails. A selector or property missing from the site's styles is a failure, never a skip. `content` entries are skipped and counted.
- Exit 0 only when `rest` and `motion` have zero failures.
- Smoke test before you use it, saved to `WS/gates/strict-smoke.txt`, with exactly these six cases: `transform-origin` `10px 20px` against `10px 60px` (must fail); `box-shadow` `rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.5) 0px 4px 12px 0px` against `rgba(0, 255, 255, 1) 0px 40px 90px 30px` (must fail); `opacity` `1` against `0` (must fail); `transition-duration` `0.3s` against `1.2s` (must fail); `width` `400px` against `400.9px` (must pass); a selector missing from the site's styles (must fail). The upstream script passes the first four.
