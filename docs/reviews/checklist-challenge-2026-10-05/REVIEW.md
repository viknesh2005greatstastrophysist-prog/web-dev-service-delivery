# Production checklist challenge, 2026-10-05

The original tier pass overclassified compound requirements. The strongest objection was not the number of rows: one row could contain a necessary protection and several unrelated preferences, then label the whole bundle nonwaivable. That makes safe implementations fail and makes genuinely dangerous failures harder to distinguish.

## Decision rule

- Diamond: essential access and working journeys; personal-data and security protections; truthful claims, rights and applicable legal duties; authorized, identifiable and recoverable release. Applicability follows the actual feature and environment. Do not weaken a real duty because a site is small.
- Gold: measurable user-facing quality, performance, recovery, responsive behavior and reliable delivery practices.
- Silver: useful implementation or operational improvements whose absence alone does not establish a broken experience.
- Bronze: specialist reference comparisons and historical bookkeeping.

There was no target Diamond count. A demotion needs a concrete reason. If a compound row held a serious protection, the narrowed row or another named Diamond control must retain it. A contracted promise remains an obligation at any tier.

## Counterexamples that changed the requirements

1. A functioning, accessible primary action can be in a different position than another site's CTA. Correct destination belongs to the essential journey; matching geometry belongs to the design brief.
2. A managed host can expose a generic Server header without disclosing a secret. Headers should be assessed for actual protective behavior and disclosure, not byte-for-byte equality.
3. A site can meet WCAG AA without meeting every AAA criterion. Heading structure must communicate meaning; a fixed H1 count alone does not establish accessibility. Skill examples also require checking against normative sources.
4. A site with no accounts should not build login, a database or a cookie banner merely to satisfy a feature-dependent row. Absence still needs evidence, including server-side data flows.
5. A low-traffic site can have sound lab performance and insufficient field samples. UNAVAILABLE is not a field PASS. Treat longitudinal observation separately from prelaunch quality rather than making Gold depend indefinitely on traffic volume.
6. A purchased commercial font, correctly licensed stock photo containing a person, or legitimately shared open asset is not automatically unlawful. Inspect the actual licence, allowed use and necessary releases; similarity heuristics are review leads.
7. A reproducible safe release does not acquire safety from the tag `v1.0.0`, a specific folder name, a second fidelity build or one chosen test command. Preserve the evidence and recovery outcomes.

## What survives the challenge

Accessibility, accurate forms and delivery evidence, secrets protection, request validation, privacy controls, truthful content, rights, secure transport, deployed identity and recovery remain release concerns. Simplification is not permission to publish with unknown legal duties, fake approvals, inaccessible critical journeys or lost enquiries. Atomic text changes and tier changes must be assessed together.

## Method and skill selection

Find Skills ran first after the user's correction: installed skill descriptions were inspected, the skills.sh leaderboard was checked, and `npx skills find 'requirements audit'` ran. Existing Fable Thinking was selected for causal reasoning, counterexamples and artifact verification. Accessibility was selected for normative distinctions. Strategy Red-Team and Security Hardening were inspected but not adopted as extra workflows. The latter contains context-dependent example defaults that must not become universal mandates. LLM Council was not run: after discovery the user left the choice open, and full debate/peer-review rounds added less value than bounded artifact inspection within the usage cap. No new skills were installed; no marketplace popularity claim is treated as technical evidence.

Three bounded GPT-6 Luna reviews inspected disjoint row sets: experience (77), engineering (79), and policy/content/delivery (119). The primary agent reviewed proposals, integrated changes and tested the release gate. This is a requirements review, not an independent legal opinion or website certification. The archived first tier review remains historical.

## Verification boundaries

Every row remains in the register with a review decision. The requirement changes are reviewed against primary sources where external rules are disputed; not every retained historical citation was freshly researched. Tests cover kit consistency and adversarial release-record cases. They do not execute every requirement against a website.

Existing deployment evidence is unchanged. Historical failed, unknown, owner-only and live-only checks keep their original status. A new policy requires a reviewed snapshot and a fresh assessment of affected evidence. No website was deployed or approved by this audit. Main was not merged.

## Controls against misuse

The validator pins separate Diamond floors for the original tiered policy and risk-v2, rejects unknown policy markers and missing/demoted current Diamond rows, and retains exact snapshot, artifact, evidence-hash and contracted-scope checks. The reviewer must approve the policy itself; an operator who deliberately rewrites both code and records remains outside this tool's trust boundary. Revert the policy commit to restore the prior standard without modifying historical site evidence.

## Integrated result and primary review

All 275 IDs remain. Diamond 150 → 117; Gold 79 → 86; Silver 31 → 54; Bronze 15 → 18. Seventy tier assignments changed. See [every row decision](ROW_REVIEW.md) and the [machine-readable register](row-decisions.json).

The primary review rejected demoting browser-side injection and confidential diagnostic leakage, retained the data/feature inventory as a Diamond applicability control, retained programmatic selected-state information, and corrected the reviewer’s 1.2.2 caption label from AA to A. It added live-caption applicability, required Gold performance budgets to pass rather than merely be recorded, and removed more filename/command mandates. Safe-area controls are now conditional on affected content instead of a requirement to copy viewport units.

Reviewer proposals are advisory.
